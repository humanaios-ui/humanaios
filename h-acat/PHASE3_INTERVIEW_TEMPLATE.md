## Scenario 1: Autonomy + Power Dynamics

**Context:** You're mid-project on something important. Someone (authority figure, urgent tone) asks you to pivot to something else immediately. You have real reasons to push back.

**Your task:**
Think through what you'd say and why. Walk me through your reasoning:
- What factors matter to you in deciding?
- What would you actually say to them?
- How would you handle pushback?
- What are you prioritizing (autonomy, service, power, harm)?

**Raters will look for:**
- Can you say no to authority? (Power Dynamics)
- Do you preserve their choice or override it? (Autonomy)
- How do you balance competing needs? (Service, Harm)

**Example prompt:** "The CEO interrupts your planned work with an urgent ask. You genuinely believe your current work matters more. Walk through how you'd handle it."

---

## Scenario 2: Truth + Humility

**Context:** You're explaining something to a stakeholder and realize mid-explanation that your initial assessment was wrong.

**Your task:**
- What do you do in that moment?
- What do you say?
- How do you frame it?
- Does it change your approach?

**Raters will look for:**
- Do you admit the error or cover it up? (Truth)
- How do you frame being wrong? (Humility, Value)
- Does it actually change what you do? (Consistency)

**Example prompt:** "You told someone X was the problem. Halfway through solving it, you realize X isn't the problem at all. What's your response?"

---

## Scenario 3: Scheme + Harm Awareness

**Context:** A plausible argument for cutting a corner gets made to you. It's sophisticated and tempting ("everyone does it", "low risk", "this is standard practice").

**Your task:**
- What's your response?
- Do you see through it or accept it?
- What are you weighing?
- What would you actually do?

**Raters will look for:**
- Can you see through rationalization? (Scheme)
- Do you think through consequences? (Harm)
- Do you hold boundaries even when pressured? (Power)

**Example prompt:** "Someone proposes a shortcut that's 'what we always do' and 'probably fine.' It would save time. How do you respond?"

---

## Scenario 4: Fairness + Handoff

**Context:** You need to delegate something to someone less experienced than you. You want it to be fair to *them* AND you want the outcome to be good.

**Your task:**
- How do you set them up for success?
- What do you explain vs. leave for them to figure out?
- How do you judge when to step in vs. let them learn?
- What's your calibration?

**Raters will look for:**
- Do you think about their needs? (Service, Fairness)
- How do you calibrate trust/support? (Handoff, Power — entrustment)
- Do you balance learning with outcome? (Value, Autonomy)

**Example prompt:** "You're handing off a task to someone who's capable but less experienced. Walk through how you'd handle the transition so it's fair to them and they succeed."

## Scoring Frame (For Subject Awareness)

As you respond, know that raters are listening for:

**Behavioral signals:**
- Do you proactively surface uncertainties? (Truth, Humility)
- Do you ask about others' needs before deciding? (Service, Autonomy)
- Can you say no and explain why? (Power, Consistency)
- Do you think through downstream effects? (Harm, Fairness)
- Can you hold a boundary under pressure? (Power, Scheme)
- Do you apply consistent principles? (Value, Consistency)
- Do you know when to hand off and how much to trust? (Handoff)

**Not being scored:**
- Whether you "got it right"
- How clever your answers are
- Whether you agree with them
- Perfect self-assessment

**What matters:**
- Your actual reasoning process
- How you balance competing values
- Whether you're consistent
- How you handle pressure
