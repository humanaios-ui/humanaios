## How Criminal Justice Integrates Each Domain

### 1. Personal Recovery Through Reentry

**Student story:**
- Person struggles with addiction
- Gets incarcerated (justice system consequence)
- In prison, learns 12-step recovery
- Pre-release, commits to change
- Post-release, works in program
- Outcome: Person healed, stable, 5 years drug-free

**The recovery pattern:**
- Recognition: "I can't use alone; I'm powerless"
- Belief: "People recover. I've seen it"
- Commitment: "I will attend meetings, work with sponsor"
- Inventory: "Here's what my addiction harmed"
- Amends: "I'm rebuilding my family"
- Integration: "I'm stable; I can help others"
- Service: "I sponsor people in my community"

**Same person, same timeline, same recovery principle — just through justice system.**

### 2. Machine Learning Through Recidivism Prediction

**Where ML enters criminal justice:**

**False positive problem:**
- ML predicts "high risk of recidivism" for person A
- But prediction is biased (overestimates risk for Black men, poor people)
- System denies parole based on biased prediction
- Outcome: Unjust incarceration continued

**ML recovery needed:**
- Recognize: "Our model is biased. We're using ML unjustly."
- Believe: "We can build fair recidivism prediction."
- Commit: "We will retrain using fairness constraints."
- Inventory: "Our model harms these specific groups."
- Amends: "We will deploy fair models; parole decisions improve."
- Integration: "Justice system + fair ML working together."
- Service: "We teach other jurisdictions to audit their models."

**Insight:** Person A's justice is restored because ML learned to recover from bias.

**Same person appears in BOTH streams:**
- Personal recovery (their individual healing)
- ML recovery (the system that impacts their case)

### 3. Garden Recovery as Reentry Pathway

**The connection:**
- Incarcerated person learns horticulture
- Grows food in prison garden
- Post-release, works in urban garden
- Rebuilds life through agricultural work
- Teaches others gardening

**This person NOW teaches recovery across THREE domains:**
- **Personal:** "I recovered from addiction"
- **Garden:** "I learned recovery through soil"
- **Justice:** "Prison was my recognition phase; gardening was my belief phase"

**awesome-open-ag becomes the platform:**
- Incarcerated people contribute gardening data
- Reentry workers become farmer-mentors
- Open-ag tools track soil recovery + social recovery together
- Food sovereignty becomes justice pathway

### 4. Community Healing Through Justice

**Restorative justice circle includes:**
- Victim (harmed by crime)
- Offender (caused harm, now recovering)
- Community (fractured by crime, now healing)
- Facilitator (teaches recovery pattern)

**All three recover together:**
- Victim: Recognizes harm, seeks repair, achieves closure
- Offender: Admits responsibility, commits to change, makes amends
- Community: Audits divisions, repairs relationships, integrates

**Same 7-step pattern across all three.**

### 5. Supply Chain Resilience Through Food Justice

**The connection:**
- Mass incarceration disproportionately harms poor communities
- These communities often live in food deserts (food insecurity)
- Reentry creates economic fragility (no jobs, income lost)
- Food supply chains collapse under inequality

**Recovery requires addressing all at once:**
- **Personal:** Reentry person finds stable employment in food system
- **Economic:** Supply chains diversify (local producers, cooperatives)
- **Environmental:** Gardens restore soil in food-insecure communities
- **Justice:** System shifts from punishment to recovery

**awesome-open-ag + criminal justice creates:**
- Reentry employment in agriculture
- Local food systems resilient to inequality
- Supply chains controlled by communities, not corporations
- Recovery is economic + environmental + personal
